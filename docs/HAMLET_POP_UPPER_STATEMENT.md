# Hamlet Pop-Upper — work statement

### Remember this answer.

Father is dead before *Hamlet Pop-Upper* asks its first question. Inside a single browser tab, Hamlet's sentence enters the procedure of a popup: it interrupts, takes the foreground, waits for a selection, withdraws, and returns. The popup becomes a second performer, carrying the question from one call to the next.

"Remember this answer" gives a selection a future. "Ask me later" gives the question one. The first brings a previous choice into a later encounter; the second composes an interval before the next demand. Both are ordinary ways interfaces organize decisions. Here they become parts of a miniature dramaturgy, placed beside a question whose answer is remade by the time of asking.

A fixed score of deaths advances alongside the returning popup. The score supplies a time already decided; the visitor supplies choices made in changing moments. Their relation produces the work's variations. Each event reaches the apparatus through the condition left by an answer, a memory, or a deferral. The score keeps its order, while the trace records how those two times met.

The adaptation takes place through the popup's operation of recurrence. The question returns as language; the popup returns as a scheduled call and stored state. When these temporal forms occupy the same apparatus, a selected value can remain exact as data while the next encounter renews, weakens, or reverses the person's commitment to it. *Hamlet Pop-Upper* keeps that interval active: the answer arrives, the work carries it forward, and the question acquires another present.

Mohammad Zare (Mozare)
